## Description

Full text below, 3,884 characters. First three lines are what shows above the fold.

---

Every tag, license and deadline for every state you hunt, in one place.

Seasons, tags, licenses, hunt regs, draw applications and preference points — organized
around the year you actually hunt, with reminders before every opener, deadline and
expiry. Nothing to sign up for and nothing to maintain.

It never claims to know your season dates or your regulations. It stores what you
entered, keeps the agency's own link beside it, and tells you when you last read it.

Free to use. No account, no ads, and the hunt journal is never paywalled.

HOW IT WORKS

1. Add a season — one species, one place, one year. California Deer, 2026.
2. Hang everything off it: the tag, the license that covers it, the dates it runs, the
   official regulation link, and who is hunting it.
3. Today assembles itself. Openers count down, licenses warn you before they lapse, and
   draw deadlines arrive 30, 7 and 1 days out.

WHO IT IS FOR

• Multi-state hunters juggling licenses, tags and validations across three states
• Western hunters tracking draw applications, results dates and preference points
• Anyone who has ever found out at a check station that something expired
• Families who hunt together, including the people who will never install an app
• Hunters who keep a journal and want the blank days in it too

WHAT YOU GET

Never miss the deadline you cannot get back — draw applications move through planning,
applied, drawn, tag purchased, with reminders at 30, 7 and 1 days and preference points
tracked year over year.

Know who is actually ready before you drive four hours — trips check every person going
against the trip's own dates, so a license that lapses the day before the opener shows
up on a Tuesday instead of in a parking lot.

Read the regulations before the opener, not after something goes wrong — save the
agency's official link and your own notes, mark when you last reviewed it, and get a
nudge two weeks out.

Enter a license once, not four times — one state hunting license points at deer,
turkey, upland and waterfowl. Tags, permits and validations stay separate, because they
are separate.

Every state and every province — all 50 states, federal stamps, and Canada, with the
wording each one uses.

A hunt journal that keeps the whole season — where you went, what you carried, what came
of it, with photographs. Days with nothing taken are kept with the same weight, because
most days are those.

Your year, in one page — days afield, harvests, states, methods, who you went with, and
the firsts. Export it as a PDF or share it as an image.

Everything on your device — no account, no server, works with no signal, and you can
export all of it to a file whenever you want.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Does it work offline?
Yes. Everything is stored on your iPhone, so tags, dates, notes and your journal work
with no signal. Only opening a regulation link needs a connection, because that goes to
the agency's own site.

Is there a subscription?
The app is free, including an unlimited journal, unlimited photos, reminders and three
active licenses, tags or permits. Outdoorsman ($19.99/year) removes the limit and adds
draws, preference points, calendar sync and export. Family ($34.99/year) adds up to six
people and trip readiness. Cancel and nothing is deleted.

Is this an official license I can show a warden?
No. Documents you store are reference copies for your own use. Follow your state or
province's rules for what has to be carried or displayed.

Add your first season today, and let the app remember the rest.

Hunting Tags & License Tracker is an independent record-keeping app, not affiliated with
any state, provincial or federal wildlife agency. It does not issue or replace licenses,
tags or permits and is not a source of regulations or season dates. Always verify current
regulations, dates and carry requirements with the agency that issues them.

## Notes on choices

- **"One-time purchase" does not apply.** The standard reassurance line had to be
  replaced with what is actually true: free to use, no account, journal never paywalled.
  Claiming no subscription on a subscription app is a refund request and a one-star
  review.
- **The credibility anchor is inverted.** Other apps cite a data edition. This one has no
  regulation data on purpose, so the trust line is that it never pretends to — which is
  more reassuring to a hunter who has been burned by a wrong date in an app.
- **Features are ordered by purchase intent**, not by tab order. Draws and trip readiness
  are the two things worth paying for, so they lead.
- **No exclamation marks**, per the voice rules. For this audience, flat statements read
  as more credible than enthusiasm.
